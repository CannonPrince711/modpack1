# CREDITS AND LICENSES — Saltglass 1.0.0

Saltglass is a curated modpack. **Every mod in it belongs to its authors, is distributed by its
authors on Modrinth or CurseForge, and is downloaded from there — no jar is bundled, rehosted,
mirrored or edited by this pack.** The pack is a manifest, a configuration set, a datapack and a
quest book, and those four things are the pack's own work.

### Notes on the two All-Rights-Reserved entries

* **FTB Quests / Library / Teams** are distributed only through CurseForge and are included in the
  pack manifest as *references to the authors' own files*, which is how every FTB-based pack
  distributes them. The FTB mods are not bundled, not rehosted and not modified.
* **Xaero's Minimap** is ARR and source-available. It is referenced, never redistributed; the
  manifest points at the author's own Modrinth file with a pinned version.
* **Nothing in this pack is uploaded anywhere.** The exports are complete only after the intended
  launcher resolves each entry from the authors' own CDNs. This is also why the `.mrpack` and the
  CurseForge tree contain **no `mods/*.jar` payload** — see `TEST_REPORT.md` §1.

### Explicitly not included

**OptiFine is not included and is not supported.** The pack cannot verify it against
Embeddium + Oculus + Create 6 + Ars Nouveau as a stack, and it replaces render code that Embeddium
already replaces. Shaders are supported *optionally* through Oculus and no shaderpack is shipped.

## 3. Attribution in-game

The quest book's reference chapter ("The Salvage-Priests' Notes") refers to the pack's own rules, and
`docs/` inside every export contains this file and `MODLIST.md`, which names every mod, its author
and its version. A player who installs Saltglass receives the attribution list with the pack.

---

## 4. If you are a mod author

If anything here misstates your licence, or if you would prefer your mod not appear in a pack like
this, that is entirely your call — the fix is one line in `pack/mods.lock.json` and a rebuild, and
the affected content is designed to degrade gracefully (the pack's spine is its own datapack, not any
single dependency).
